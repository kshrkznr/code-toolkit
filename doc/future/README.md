# Future

Future documents preserve unsettled candidate concepts and directions that may
become useful to CTK.

They keep a possibility visible without presenting it as accepted
responsibility, an implementation plan, or a roadmap commitment.

## Responsibility

A Future document should make the candidate, current evidence, unresolved
questions, and conditions for reconsideration visible enough that later work
can decide whether the direction has become relevant.

Future is a destination of review, not a required stage after Note or before
Core. A candidate may later become an Experiment, Design Note, accepted
Knowledge, or be dropped when evidence changes.

## Navigate by question

Start with the question closest to the current work:

- Which broader candidate directions are currently collected for later
  review?
  → [Collected Future Candidates](future.candidates.md)
- When would a generic Capability Ingredient Layer become clearer than Runtime,
  Profile, or explicit Recipe composition?
  → [Capability Layer](future.capability-layer.md)

These routes are entry points rather than an implementation queue. Search by
the candidate concept when a future document is not yet listed here.

## Boundary

Future documents do not define:

- accepted Concept API responsibilities
- required behavior or representation Contracts
- scheduled implementation work
- a promise that a candidate will be adopted
- conclusions that have not yet been supported by observation

Use the [Documentation Resolver](../README.md) for current accepted
responsibilities. Use an Experiment when the thought path, procedure, and
observations are the material that needs to be preserved.

## Local guidance

A Future title should expose the candidate without making it sound accepted.
Current evidence, open questions, boundaries, priority, and reconsideration
conditions are useful when they clarify why the candidate remains open.

Keep speculative detail proportional to current evidence. Prefer recording the
condition that would justify another review over designing an implementation
before its responsibility is clear.
