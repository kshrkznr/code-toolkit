# Naming

Project-specific terminology should prioritize consistent responsibility over
strict conformity with a dictionary meaning.

When a new responsibility no longer fits the existing vocabulary, consider
introducing a new term.

---

Naming follows the discovery of responsibility.

Give an emerging responsibility a provisional name, then observe whether the
name settles through use. Prioritize whether the responsibility has been
separated appropriately over whether the initial name is exact.

## Names are validated through conversation

CTK rarely tries to find the perfect name in advance.

A temporary label is first made good enough for discussion. It is then used in
ordinary conversation and changed when it repeatedly produces hesitation or a
different image between participants. If weeks of use pass without anyone
naturally replacing it, the label may become the project term.

```text
Temporary label
      ↓
Use in conversation
      ↓
Observe ambiguity or mismatch
      ↓
Rename or let the term settle
```

In this sense, names are validated through use rather than brainstorming. The
practical question is not only whether a word matches its dictionary meaning,
but whether the conversation becomes unclear when the word is used.

Many CTK terms began as ordinary English words. Repeated use gradually gave
them project-specific meanings. The ordinary meaning remains a useful Analogy
and an entrance to the term, but it does not fully define the CTK Vocabulary.

This is a loose observation about how naming has worked in CTK, not a required
naming process.

---

> **Document Naming Convention**
>
> Document names are composed from left to right, from broader scope to more specific scope.
>
> ```
> <domain>.<kind>.<subject>.md
> ```
